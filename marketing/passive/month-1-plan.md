# Passive income — Month 1 (Sept 2026)

Written 2026-08-28. This is **month 1 of a 6-month track**, and the honest
headline is up front so no one plans around a fantasy.

## What this month will actually earn

**Roughly $0.** Possibly a first affiliate click; a first sale would be
lucky. Anything promising real passive revenue in 30 days from a standing
start with no budget and no audience is lying to you.

What month 1 *can* do is fix the thing actually blocking every passive
model, and lay rails so money can flow when traffic arrives.

Honest projection, zero budget:

| | Realistic | Good outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Month 1 | $0 | $0–10 |
| Month 3 | $0–25 | $50 |
| Month 6 | $25–100 | $200–300 |

**For comparison, stated once and then dropped:** one East Coast Social
client is $79/month recurring and is reachable in weeks, not months. This
plan is worth running *alongside* that, not instead of it. The passive
track's value is that it compounds while you sleep; ECS's value is that it
pays now.

## The diagnosis this plan is built on

All-time traffic data (2026-08-28): 215 visitors ever, ~3/day. Every
channel measured: Facebook group ad 6 clicks, ECS caption 1, **Pinterest 0,
TikTok 0, X 0, every QR code 0.** One contact tap, ever.

Every passive model — store sales, affiliate, ads — is a conversion rate
multiplied by traffic. At three visitors a day, no new income stream
changes anything. **Traffic is the bottleneck, so this month is mostly a
traffic plan with monetisation attached.**

## The structural problem nobody had named

The store rotates its entire catalogue every three days. No product page
ever accumulates search authority, so **findhotstuff.com is architecturally
incapable of ranking on Google.** It was built for freshness; freshness and
SEO are opposites. Fixing that is week 1's engineering job.

## The one rule that protects the whole thing

AI drafts everything here, but **no mass-generated filler gets published.**
Google's spam policies target "scaled content abuse" — mass-produced pages
made mainly to rank — and enforcement deindexes whole domains. That would
kill the ECS proof asset along with the store.

So: AI writes from *Matthew's actual experience*, Matthew verifies every
factual claim, and nothing publishes that he hasn't read. Fewer, better,
true. The competitive edge here is real: nobody else can mass-produce "I
built this and here's what broke," and that's exactly the content that
earns links and trust.

# The four tracks

## Track A — Content that compounds *(the main event)*

The asset: Matthew genuinely built and operates an automated store and
service. That story, told concretely, is rankable, linkable, and
monetisable — and it is not reproducible by a content farm.

Targets are long-tail queries a zero-authority site can actually win:
specific, practical, low-competition. Not "make money online."

**Ten pieces, published across the month**, each built the same way:
Claude drafts from a real thing that happened → Matthew reads and corrects
→ publish at `findhotstuff.com/notes/<slug>` → linked from `/build`.

Seed list (all drawn from things that genuinely happened):
1. What it costs to run an automated store at $0/month — the actual stack
2. The wallpaper that got sold as a pet bed: why I built a validator that
   can reject my own product names
3. Pricing dropshipped goods so no sale can lose money — the three costs
   people forget
4. Getting TikTok's Content Posting API to accept photo posts (JPEG twins,
   no emoji in titles, and the other undocumented parts)
5. Why ntfy from a Cloudflare Worker gets rate-limited, and the GitHub
   relay that fixes it
6. A daily-posting engine that has run N days without me — architecture
7. What one Facebook group post taught me that three weeks of DMs didn't
8. Google Business Profile video verification, start to finish, for a
   service-area business
9. Catalog rotation vs SEO: the tradeoff I didn't understand when I built it
10. Running all of this around a full-time job

## Track B — Pinterest at volume *(the fastest traffic)*

Pinterest is the only channel where a post keeps working for months, and
the pin generator already exists. Currently: sporadic posting, 0 clicks.

- Target **5 pins/day, every day** — 150 for the month
- Claude regenerates packs and preps titles/descriptions; Matthew publishes
- Boards: Trending Finds (products), Small Business Marketing (ECS),
  plus one new board per content piece from Track A
- **Honest expectation: nothing for 60–90 days.** Pinterest rewards
  consistency and age. If it hasn't moved by month 3, kill it.

## Track C — Affiliate rails *(monetisation, sequenced carefully)*

**Do NOT apply to Amazon Associates yet.** Amazon.ca closes accounts that
don't produce **3 qualifying sales within 180 days** of applying. Applying
now, at 3 visitors/day, burns the application. Apply in **week 4 of month
2**, once Track A has traffic — the clock should start when there's an
audience to convert.

Start instead with programs that have no sales gate and pay recurring:
- Tools genuinely in use here and worth recommending honestly
- Anything Matthew wouldn't recommend to a friend is not listed, ever

**Disclosure is mandatory and non-negotiable** — Canada's Competition
Bureau requires material connections be disclosed clearly. A standard
disclosure block goes at the top of any page carrying affiliate links,
before the first link, not buried in a footer.

## Track D — Fix the store's SEO architecture *(week 1 engineering)*

The rotation destroys ranking. The fix isn't to stop rotating — freshness
is the store's whole idea — it's to add stable ground beside it:

- Evergreen category pages (`/kitchen/`, `/pet/`, …) that persist while
  their contents rotate
- A stable "what's been popular" page that doesn't churn
- Proper titles/descriptions/schema on both
- Keep the rotating grid exactly as it is

That gives Google something permanent to index while the shelf stays fresh.

# The 30 days

## Week 1 — Foundations (Sept 1–7)
**Claude:** build `/notes/` section + templates; build the evergreen
category pages (Track D); draft content pieces 1–3; write the affiliate
disclosure block; assemble the keyword target list.
**Matthew:** read and correct pieces 1–3 (~20 min each); publish 5 pins/day;
keep ECS prospecting running.
**Milestone:** 3 pieces live, store SEO structure fixed, 35 pins out.

## Week 2 — Volume (Sept 8–14)
**Claude:** draft pieces 4–6; submit sitemap to Google Search Console;
build an internal-linking pass.
**Matthew:** verify and publish; 5 pins/day; **set up Google Search
Console** (his clicks, ~10 min) — without it we're flying blind.
**Milestone:** 6 pieces live, Search Console reporting, 70 pins out.

## Week 3 — Distribution (Sept 15–21)
**Claude:** draft pieces 7–9; prep sharing copy for each piece for the
local groups and relevant communities.
**Matthew:** verify and publish; 5 pins/day; share pieces where they're
genuinely welcome — no spamming.
**Milestone:** 9 pieces live, first impressions visible in Search Console.

## Week 4 — Measure and prune (Sept 22–30)
**Claude:** draft piece 10; pull a full channel report; recommend what to
kill.
**Matthew:** verify and publish; decide what continues into month 2.
**Milestone:** 10 pieces live, ~150 pins, honest read on what moved.

## The kill criteria, set now while it's unemotional

- **Pinterest:** zero clicks by end of month 3 → stop.
- **Any content piece** with zero impressions after 90 days → don't write
  more like it.
- **The whole track:** if month 3 ends under $25 *and* under 300 monthly
  visitors, the honest conclusion is that this is a 12-month project, not a
  6-month one — decide then whether it's still worth the evenings.

## What this plan deliberately does not include

- Buying traffic (no budget, and it isn't passive)
- New marketplaces (Fiverr/Upwork are active income and already tried)
- Mass AI content farms (deindex risk, and it's dishonest)
- Fake reviews or invented social proof — same rule as everything else here
