## Self-Employment Benefit (SEB) — New Brunswick

**Official guide:** New Brunswick Self-Employment Benefit Program – Guide for Applicant (November 1, 2025). [https://www.pxw1.snb.ca/snb7001/e/1000/CSS-FOL-61-6277-06E_GUIDE.pdf]

**What it is:** Helps unemployed individuals create a job for themselves by starting a new business. Provides income support plus counselling/technical advice through the local CBDC. Not a lump-sum grant.

**Financial assistance:**
- If receiving or eligible for EI: continue receiving EI until the claim ends. After that, support at a provincially established rate for the remaining time on the benefit.
- If approved but not on EI: weekly allowance at a provincially established rate.
- Business revenue is not deducted from benefits during participation. Tax clawback may apply if income exceeds certain levels (check with CRA).
- Exact weekly amount and total duration: UNKNOWN from public sources (depends on EI entitlement and provincial rate; local SEB Coordinator confirms).

**Key eligibility (from the Nov 1 2025 guide):**
- Legally entitled to work in Canada; Canadian citizen or permanent resident.
- Residing in New Brunswick.
- Unemployed and EI eligible.
- Present a business plan.
- Not a full-time student.
- Must not have started the business before applying, or before approval and signing a contract.
- Must not have received SEB funding in the last five years.
- Decision-making control; at least 51% ownership (formal agreement).
- Family-owned similar businesses reviewed case-by-case.
- Agree to credit review.
- Provide at least 5% of total start-up costs in cash or in-kind.
- Work full-time (at least 35 hours/week).

**Business requirements:**
- For-profit, viable, full-time year-round operation.
- Established in NB; begin operation within 10 weeks of signing the SEB contract.
- Franchise fee ≤ $50,000 eligible. Commission sales, pyramid, network/MLM not eligible.
- Must not be in undue competition with existing ventures or exploit vulnerable groups.

**Process (high level):**
1. Meeting with local SEB Coordinator (CBDC) to assess suitability.
2. Complete application and prepare business plan (background, marketing, production if needed, financing, legal form, forecasts, risk).
3. Coordinator presents to a committee for decision against provincial criteria.

**Note on sources:** Some secondary sites (GrantHub etc.) state EI within the past 36 months or non-EI allowance options. The official Nov 1 2025 guide emphasizes “unemployed and EI eligible.” Confirm current rules with the local coordinator.

## CBDC Westmorland Albert

**Location:** 337 Main Street, Shediac, NB E4P 2B1. Phone (506) 532-8312. Hours Mon–Fri 8:30am–4:30pm. [https://cbdc.ca/locations/cbdc-westmorland-albert/]

**Loan programs listed (amounts up to $150,000 where stated; programs may vary by region):**
- First Time Entrepreneur Loan: start or buy a first business.
- General Business Loan: up to $150,000 for start, expand, or improve (rural Atlantic).
- Impact Loan Program: Moncton, Dieppe, Riverview; fast approval, flexible terms, no early payment penalties. Amount UNKNOWN on the page.
- Innovation Loan: up to $150,000 for new technology/productivity.
- Social Enterprise Loan: up to $150,000 for non-profits.
- Youth Loan Program: up to $150,000 for ages 18–34.

SEB is delivered through CBDCs; the location page does not list SEB as a separate loan product. Contact the office for current SEB availability and process.
