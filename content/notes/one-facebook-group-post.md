I run a small local service business alongside an automated store. For
about six weeks I pushed content to every channel I could automate:
Pinterest pins, TikTok carousels, posts on X, QR codes on printed
materials.

Here is the complete tracked-click ledger for all of it, pulled today from
my analytics:

```
  Pinterest            0
  TikTok               0
  X                    0
  Every QR code        0
  One Facebook group   6
```

Six clicks is not a success story. I want to be clear about that before I
draw any lesson from it — this is a tiny number from a tiny business and
you should weight it accordingly.

But it is the *only* non-zero number I have, and the shape of it changed
what I do.

## What the working one actually was

A written post in a local buy-and-sell style Facebook group. Not an ad.
Not a link drop. A description of what I was doing, posted where the
people it might be relevant to already were, written to be read rather
than clicked.

It took about fifteen minutes and cost nothing.

## Why I think the others got zero

Not because those platforms do not work. Because **I had no audience on
them, and I was using them as broadcast channels rather than places where
anyone was already standing.**

- Pinterest pins go into a search index where a new account with no
  history is invisible. Mine were also static images at a moment when the
  platform was pushing video hard.
- TikTok carousels get almost no distribution compared with video, which
  I found out after building the carousel pipeline.
- The QR codes were on printed material that had not yet been handed to
  enough people for a scan to be likely.

In every case I built a distribution mechanism before I had checked
whether distribution existed. The Facebook group did not need building. It
already had the people in it.

## The thing I got wrong, stated plainly

I automated the channels that were easy to automate rather than the
channels that were likely to work. Those are not the same list, and the
overlap is smaller than you would hope.

Pins and carousels can be generated by a script. A post in a local group
has to be written by a person who is a member of that group, which is
exactly why it is not commoditised — and why it was the only thing that
moved.

## What I would do differently

**Find the room before you build the megaphone.** For anything local, the
room is almost certainly a Facebook group, a community board, or a
newsletter somebody else already runs, and getting into it costs
conversation rather than code.

And **instrument everything from day one.** The reason I can write this
piece at all is that every link carries a tag and I can say "zero" with
confidence instead of guessing. Six clicks is a disappointing number, but
knowing it is six and not forty is worth more than the forty would have
been.

I will update this page when I have a bigger sample. If the numbers change
the conclusion, the conclusion changes.
