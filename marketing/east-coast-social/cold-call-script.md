# The phone script — word for word, for when your heart is going

`call-kit.md` has the strategy: when to call, the objection table, the
ledger. This is the other thing — the words, in order, big enough to read
off a phone while it's ringing. Nobody freezes because they forgot the
strategy. They freeze because they don't know the next sentence.

**Read the bold lines out loud once before you dial.** That's the whole prep.

---

## Before you dial (20 seconds)

1. Stand up. You sound different sitting down.
2. Have their page open, or know it doesn't exist. One specific detail —
   *"quiet since April"*, *"no page at all"* — is what makes this a call
   from a neighbour instead of a telemarketer.
3. Decide the ask **before** you dial. It is never "buy this." It is
   **"can I build you a free week and show you?"**

Times that work: **shops & services 10:00–11:30 AM. Restaurants 2:00–4:00
PM, never during a rush.** Skip Monday mornings.

---

## When somebody picks up

> **"Hi — is this the owner?"**

*(If not: "No worries — when's he in? I'll call back, it's a thirty-second
thing." Get a name and a time. Write it down. Hang up. Don't pitch a
teenager.)*

> **"Hey [name], it's Matthew — I'm from Memramcook, so basically a
> neighbour. Thirty seconds and I'll let you go, promise."**

Stop. Let them say okay. That pause is what separates you from a robocall.

> **"I noticed [your page has been quiet since April / you don't have a
> Facebook page at all]. I run a little local thing that puts a post on a
> business's page every single day, automatically — you approve the look
> once and then never touch it."**

> **"My own store's feed has published a new post every single day since
> August 4th. You can go look at it right now — findhotstuff.com/automation.
> That's the whole sales pitch, honestly: go look."**

> ⚠️ **Check the page before you dial.** The counter line at the top says
> how many days it's been. If that line is *missing*, the streak broke —
> say nothing about it and sell the free week instead. Never quote a
> number you haven't just looked at. And it's the **site feed**, never
> "my Facebook page" — those are different things and one of them was
> quiet for three weeks this summer.

> **"Setup's free, it's seventy-nine a month, no contract. And before any of
> that I'll build you a free week of sample posts with your own name and
> your own photos, so you can see it before you decide anything."**

> **"Can I do that for you? Costs you nothing and takes me a couple of
> evenings."**

Then **shut up.** Let it be awkward. The first person to talk loses.

---

## The three answers you'll actually get

**"Yeah, sure, send me something."**
> **"I'll do you one better — give me two days and I'll build the real
> thing with your name on it. What's your email, and is there a photo of
> the place you like?"**
Then log the date you'll be back. A "send me something" you don't follow
up on is a no you gave yourself.

**"How much again?"**
> **"Seventy-nine a month, and setup's free. For scale — an agency charges
> a thousand-plus for the same daily posting, because someone there is
> writing and designing it by hand every morning. A post a day is about
> five hours a month of somebody's time. I built the tool that does the
> making, so I'm not charging you for the hours."**

**"I'm not interested."**
> **"No problem at all — thanks for the thirty seconds. If the page ever
> goes quiet and it starts bugging you, I'm around."**
Mark it ❌ and never call again. A no handled gracefully in Memramcook is
still marketing.

---

## The line that closes the nervous ones

Save this for when they hesitate — when you can hear them thinking *"a
stranger posting on my page under my name."* That is the real fear, and it
is never about the technology.

> **"And here's the part nobody else will tell you: text me the word STOP
> and it stops. That day. No reason needed, no notice, no month's warning.
> A death in the family, a flood, a bad week — your page goes quiet until
> you text me GO. It's your name on it, so you keep the brake."**

It's on the page under *"What if I need it to stop?"* — send them there if
they want to read it themselves. **This promise is real: don't soften it,
and don't add conditions to it on the phone.**

---

## If they ask "is this AI?"

Say yes to the true part, plainly. Do not get defensive and do not
over-deny — the page fact-checks both.

> **"The posting is automated, yeah — that's exactly why it's seventy-nine
> and not a thousand. It's not writing your posts out of thin air, it's a
> system I built that puts your specials and your photos out on a schedule.
> You approve the style once before anything goes live. And you're talking
> to the guy who built it, who lives ten minutes away."**

---

## Voicemail (15 seconds, and mean it)

> **"Hi, this is Matthew from Memramcook. I help local businesses keep their
> Facebook page posting every day, automatically — setup's free and the
> first week of samples is free to look at. Have a peek at
> findhotstuff.com/automation if you're curious. Or text me, (506) 889-9737.
> Thanks!"**

Two voicemails, max. Then it's a walk-in or it's nothing.

---

## Not a cold call: the two that are already warm

**LeBlanc Restaurant, 583 rue Centrale.** He's had your printed pack since
Aug 14. Walk in 2–4. Don't re-pitch — he's already seen it.
> **"Hey — Matthew, from a few weeks back, I left you the pack. Not here to
> bug you. Just wondered if you had any thoughts on it, and whether you
> want me to run your free week for real. Costs you nothing either way."**

**CAVOK Brewing.** The owner asked you to **text**, and asking twice is
worse than asking once. Send this, don't call:
> Hi — Matthew from East Coast Social. You'd asked me to text you about the
> free week of sample posts. Totally fine if the timing's off, just didn't
> want to leave you hanging. My own store's feed has posted every single day
> since August 4th if you want a look: findhotstuff.com/automation

---

## After every call, no exceptions

One line in `call-kit.md` or `prospects.md`: **who, when, what they said,
when you're back.** An unlogged call is a call you'll make twice or never
make again. The ledger going quiet for three weeks is what a dead pipeline
looks like from the inside.
